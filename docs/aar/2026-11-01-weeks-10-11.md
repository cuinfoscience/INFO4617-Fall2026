## 5. Gap analysis (Q3)

### 5.1 Two sets of rules say different things about Friday in weeks 11–14 (P0)

**Root cause: contradictory.**
- **The register knew.** `course-facts.md` (2026-10-23) records that weeks 11–14 grade a portfolio checkpoint and says, of the slides' Friday frames, "Not yet changed". The hand-off says what to change.
- **The AUTHORING files, which build each week, disagree.** Both, and `AGENTS.md`, were written on 2026-10-21, before Canvas changed, and still prescribe a textbook-revision block "graded under Textbook Revisions".
- **The agent followed the file it reads when building a week.** It read `course-facts.md` only for the key-names row. So it rebuilt week 11's Friday revision block three times and never mentioned the checkpoint.
- **Why this matters.** Students were told to spend 15 minutes on something no assignment grades, and weren't reminded of one that is. The textbook received no student pull requests in weeks 10–11.
- **The fix is the instructor's decision** (what Friday's 15 minutes are now). The edit itself is mechanical once decided: one sentence in three files, and the rows of `course-facts.md`.

### 5.2 Copies of the same content drift, across sessions (P0)

**Root cause: missing (a check) and buried (a rule).**
- **The rule is there.** "After the chapter, when it changes" (`labs/AUTHORING.md`) and "When a chapter changes, check the slides that copy it" (`AGENTS.md`) are both in place.
- **Nothing tells a session that the chapter has changed.** In this sprint two sessions worked at once:
  - one on the textbook (#295, which asked in its plan for "a session that can reach the course repository");
  - this one on the course.
- **The pin to the chapter existed.** The key's note records the chapter commit it follows ("follows the textbook at commit `6f4cd3f`"), and so do the deck's comments. When #295 merged, those pins went stale. Nothing compared them with the chapter, so the instructor found the stale key two days later (request 6).
- **The second form of drift is a change applied only to the artifact a request named.** The restart steps went into the slides and the lab, because the request said "slides", but not into the chapter. The `getpass` steps went into the chapter only because the request named it.
- **Why the facts rule didn't catch it.** `AGENTS.md`'s rule lists the facts that trigger it (a grade weight, a due date, the `webdata` command, chapter code). Instructions for students, like how to set a key, weren't on the list, so the rule didn't fire.

### 5.3 The week was planned for twice the pace the class reached (P0)

**Root cause: missing (an evidence-based budget).**
- **Planned versus reached.**
  - `labs/AUTHORING.md` budgets about 135 minutes over three days, and Monday's part was planned at 50 minutes with steps 0–6.
  - The class reached the start of Step 4: about half.
  - Week 10's revisions step took more than 25 minutes against about 10 planned (the 2026-10-21 AAR).
- **The cost.** The plan of four APIs then shaped the chapter: Congress.gov got a section, a figure taken twice, and a list of endpoints. All of it came out of the lab in one day and out of the chapter in two. The instructor named the rule themselves: "At this stage, less is more."
- **Why it happened.** The budget is in minutes of steps, with no record of the pace measured in class. The chapter was widened to fit a lab plan before the first class could test it, although `labs/AUTHORING.md` says coverage is the chapter's job.

### 5.4 Course-level documents generalized one week's format (P2)

**Root cause: ambiguous.** "Default from now on" (2026-10-25) was written into `README.md` as the course's design, and the instructor reverted it (#146). Which file records a decision decides how it reads: the README states the course's design, while `AGENTS.md` and the AUTHORING files state working practice. A dated entry in a decision record would have said "from week 11, after student feedback" without turning it into course design.

### 5.5 The AAR collector misses mid-turn messages (P1, carried forward)

**Root cause: missing (in the skill).** It counted 28 "corrections" in this session's window. 26 of them were task notifications, and none of the 86 instructor messages sent while work ran were counted. In this session those messages are `queue-operation` records with `operation: "enqueue"`. This review read them directly.

### 5.6 Edits refused for a file not read since a compaction (P2)

**Root cause: buried.** There were 10 such refusals. The textbook's hand-off asks for a read after a compaction, but the course has no such note, and 14 compactions happened in this session. The cost was low: each was retried at once.

### [P0-2] A check for `labs/`, starting with the chapter pin
- **Target:** new `labs/tools/check_lab.py`; a `check-labs.yml` workflow (information only, like the deck checks); a line in `AGENTS.md`, "Checks before class".
- **Root cause:** missing.
- **Medium:** tool config. Comparing a pinned commit with the chapter's last change is mechanical.
- **Supersedes:** the 2026-10-21 AAR's P0-2, a smaller first version of it.
- **Evidence:** §5.2. Requests 1–6, and 9 lab pull requests merged with no lab CI.
- **Change:** `python3 labs/tools/check_lab.py week-NN` reports:
  1. **Students' copy:** `make_student_copy.py --check` for the week.
  2. **The chapter pin:** reads "follows the textbook at commit `X`" from the key's opening cell, and "following chapter NN at textbook `X`" from the deck's comments.   It runs in CI on pull requests that touch `labs/` or `slides/`, and never fails a build.
- **Why it works:** the instructor found the stale key by reading it. The script finds it on the next pull request, and it also catches the two broken anchors and two wrong figure numbers fixed by hand in #153.

- **Plans as hand-offs between sessions.** The textbook's `2026-10-26-week11-after-monday.md` said exactly what the course side needed. P1-2 makes reading it a step.
- **A rule the instructor gave was written down the same day:** "No gray blocks" went into `slides/common/AUTHORING.md` on 2026-10-25, and no gray block appears in week 11.
