## 5. Gap analysis (Q3)

### 5.1 — Per-section frame-count targets are contradicted, not just loose
**Root cause: contradictory.** `AUTHORING.md` states Monday=3–4, Wednesday=4–6, Friday=3 frames. Actual: Monday 4/22/—/9/4, Wednesday 12/6/15/14/7, Friday 10/11/14/3/4 (weeks 02/03/04/05/06 respectively — week-04 has no separate Monday since it merged sections). Zero of five decks comply, including the two built after the text was last touched. This isn't drift the contract hasn't caught up to yet — the numbers were likely always aspirational rather than descriptive, and nobody has ever built to them. Left as-is, this is the single line in the whole document most likely to actively mislead week-06's build toward being too short in the wrong direction (i.e., correctly hitting 25–40 *total* by front-loading Monday and starving Wednesday, rather than distributing where the chapter's content actually calls for depth).

### 5.2 — Standing-rule furniture is uncertain, not settled
**Root cause: ambiguous** (possibly premature generalization from n=2). The contract calls the four furniture items "confirmed recurring... standing rules" on the strength of week-02 and week-03 both having them. But of the two decks built *since* that claim was written, week-04 kept three of four and week-05 kept zero. Two readings are equally consistent with the evidence: (a) these items are situational — tied to whether a given week's daily note produced real questions, whether the chapter's Friday content needs an explicit submission callout, etc. — and calling them "standing rules" overclaims; or (b) week-04 and week-05 are missing something you actually want back in and haven't gotten to yet, the same way week-06 hasn't gotten its pass yet. Git history can't distinguish these, and this is exactly the kind of judgment call the method says to surface rather than resolve unilaterally — see P1-1.

### 5.3 — week-06 is unstarted, not off-voice
**Root cause: genuinely novel** (not a contract failure at all). Every completed deck went through the same lifecycle: an initial AI-assisted draft, then one or more instructor rewrite passes that added the density, images, and course-ops furniture that make it feel "current." week-06 has only had the first half of that lifecycle — it's had every *mechanical* pass (STE, matplotlib, AI-trope removal, bottomcite) but never the *expansion* pass. This is not evidence the contract is wrong or that week-06 was built carelessly; it's evidence the contract's job here (guide the next expansion) hasn't been invoked yet. Naming it precisely matters because the fix is content work sized against real examples, not a style correction.

### 5.4 — No review-comment channel exists in this project's actual workflow (side note, not a slide-content defect)
Worth flagging even though it's out of scope for aligning week-06's *content*: every PR in this window is opened and merged by the instructor against his own repo with zero review comments from anyone else. The course's own Friday-section content teaches students a claim/link/sharpen review taxonomy that the project generating that content doesn't itself practice. Not a recommendation — just worth knowing that "recurring human review comments," this method's best evidence source, will be structurally unavailable for any future AAR on this repo unless that changes.
